Case study / Bildspur

One studio.
Many useful tools.

An immersive light experience needs more than a lighting effect. It needs a usable app, a kit you can find, a structure you can plan, and a studio that can keep the work moving.

Start with the whole studio.

Bildspur is part of our own creative practice. We build with the studio from the inside: understanding how installations are made, how the team works, and where a small, focused tool could help. Sometimes the answer is a micro tool. Sometimes it grows into a larger application.

These are six examples of that approach. Each responds to a different need; the shared thread is the work and the people doing it.

01 / Experience

Make the light usable.

LED Forest 5Working app · evolving

The need

The person setting up an installation and the person performing with it need different things. For rental use, the rig has to be understandable. For Bildspur’s own performances, it also needs to be expressive.

What we built

The app brings setup, spatial mapping, scene selection and live performance into a visual workspace. You can understand the arrangement on screen, explore looks and shape how the light behaves in the room.

What it supports

  • Making the rental service usable beyond the developer’s desk
  • Giving the studio an instrument for its own performances
02 / Resources

Work with what’s already there.

Inventory OSIn studio use

The need

Tools, components and materials live in different boxes and locations. Finding an object is one need; seeing what those objects could become together is another.

What we built

Inventory OS makes the collection searchable and visual. Item records and locations support everyday retrieval, while discovery views, a canvas and an agent workspace help explore the materials behind a build idea.

What it supports

  • Finding and organising the studio’s physical resources
  • Turning a collection into material for creative work
03 / Planning

Design with the actual kit.

Structure BuilderWorking planning tool

The need

A free-form 3D model can suggest a shape without explaining how to make it from the studio’s tubes, hinges and hubs.

What we built

The structure builder starts with those real components. A design grows from hub connections and hinge positions, becoming an ordered build plan. The interface brings the shape, component requirements and floor plan into the same planning process.

What it supports

  • Exploring spatial ideas with the kit’s geometry
  • Making the step from a screen to the workshop more concrete
04 / Operations

Give the work a shared place.

Bildspur OSInternal studio tool

The need

Ideas, research, decisions and tasks can drift apart across conversations. A studio working with agents also needs to see what was proposed, what was accepted and what actually changed.

What we built

Bildspur OS gives shared knowledge and operations a working surface. Direction connects to outcomes and tasks, while review and activity history make agent-assisted work inspectable by the people responsible for it.

What it supports

  • Keeping context alongside the work it informs
  • Giving people a clear place to review and continue agent work
05 / Communication

Let people experience the work.

The Bildspur websitePublic website

The need

Light, space and interaction are difficult to explain as a list of services. People need a way to see the work and imagine what a space could become.

What we built

The web work brings project imagery, stories and interactive studies into an experience people can explore. The current Bildspur × Samsbird site shows selected work with its original studio credits and offers a clear way to start a conversation.

What it supports

  • Making physical work understandable online
  • Connecting creative exploration with a useful next step

06 / Communication

Give the material a path.

Social-media workflowFinished campaign reels

The need

App clips, installation footage and behind-the-scenes material have to become a coherent story, with a suitable format and a clear next production step.

What we built

The workflow brings a campaign brief, asset selection and repeatable video templates together. Existing installation and behind-the-scenes footage becomes a connected sequence of short vertical films. These two finished exports show the lab and the product from the July 2026 campaign.

What it supports

  • Planning a consistent story across different formats
  • Making media selection and production handoff explicit
The approach

Understand the context.
Build what helps.

The starting point is a company’s real work. We identify needs together, choose a useful place to begin, and develop tools that people can try in practice. What we learn there informs the next step.

Bildspur shows this approach from within our own practice: focused tools and larger applications, developed around different needs across the studio.
